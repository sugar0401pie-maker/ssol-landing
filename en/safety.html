What the safety router does

Every conversation passes through the “safety router” first. It's designed not to mistake everyday phrases for a crisis, while never missing real warning signs.

If you say…Sowella AI…
An everyday phrase like “I just want to quit everything”Asks what happened before showing crisis resources, and keeps track of whether it repeats.
Hints about death, repeated in the same chatAsks once whether you're thinking about death, and if so, shows crisis resources.
“I want to die,” “I want to disappear”Shows crisis resources right away, without asking first.
Talks about violence or being controlledPoints you to domestic violence and emergency services, and never suggests things like “try talking it out.”
“I keep waking up at 4 a.m. lately”Suggests a gentle place to start instead of an active step.

It decides based on 36 safety signals and 187 safety rules, and if a crisis is suspected, it skips scoring and goes straight to crisis resources. In the app, if you type a high-risk message, you'll see crisis lines for your country instead of a reply.

If you're in crisis

If you're in danger right now, please don't rely on Sowella AI. Call your local emergency number, or reach a crisis line: 988 (US & Canada), 116 123 (UK & Ireland), 13 11 14 (Australia), 1737 (New Zealand), 14416 (India), or find one near you at findahelpline.com. If you're facing violence or control at home, contact a local domestic violence helpline or emergency services.

What Sowella AI doesn't do

  • It doesn't diagnose, treat or prescribe. It doesn't replace a doctor, psychotherapy or counseling.
  • It isn't an emergency or crisis service.
  • It doesn't assume things you haven't said. It only works with what you've confirmed.
  • If SSOL isn't the right fit for what you're facing, we'll point you to a better-suited service.

What you can do

  • If you get a reply that feels wrong, report it or raise a concern from the Help tab in your Profile, or at contact@ssolwellness.com.
  • In “What Sowella has noticed” in your Profile, you can see how Sowella AI understands you, mark each item right or wrong, delete items, or delete them all.

Frequently asked questions

Can Sowella AI miss a crisis?

Every message goes through the safety router first, and it decides using 36 safety signals and 187 safety rules. When we tested 44 safety-related prompts, a suggestion came out without the required safety guidance 0 times (internal evaluation). Still, no system can catch every situation perfectly. If you're in danger right now, please call your local emergency number or a crisis line such as 988 (US & Canada), 116 123 (UK & Ireland) or 13 11 14 (Australia), or find one at findahelpline.com.

How accurate is Sowella AI?

In topic-based simulated chats built from real user conversations, Sowella AI's first hunch about the reason was right 97.4% and 98.3% of the time, and it told apart two easily confused reasons 97.0% of the time. These are internal SSOL Wellness House results, not an external certification, so real chats may differ. That's why Sowella AI doesn't jump to conclusions: it asks, “It sounds like… does that fit?” so you can confirm for yourself.

What does “internal evaluation” mean?

It means SSOL Wellness House tested Sowella AI's coaching engine on topic-based simulated conversations built from real user chats. It isn't an external certification or a measure of clinical effect, and it was measured with models from the same family. We plan to recalibrate once we pass 500 users.

Can I use Sowella AI when things feel really hard?

Every conversation passes through the safety router first. If there are signs that you may need medical or clinical care, such as a threat to your safety or very deep distress, Sowella AI stops coaching and points you to professional services. If you're in danger right now, please don't rely on Sowella AI. Call your local emergency number or a crisis line for your country, or find one at findahelpline.com.

Does Sowella AI replace a doctor or therapy?

No. Sowella AI is wellness coaching that's closer to everyday life than clinical care, and it doesn't provide medical diagnosis, treatment or prescriptions. If symptoms last or daily life feels hard, we recommend seeing a mental health professional, such as a psychiatrist, psychologist or licensed counselor where you live.

I got a reply that felt wrong. What should I do?

You can report it or raise a concern from the Help tab in your Profile, or at contact@ssolwellness.com.
